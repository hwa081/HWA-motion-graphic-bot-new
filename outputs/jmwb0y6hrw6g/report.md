You must have seen a spectacular catch in cricket, where a fielder jumps into the air and catches the ball just before it crosses the boundary. Now imagine replacing that ball with a spacecraft, and the catching hands with two giant steel arms. SpaceX is aiming to do exactly that on November 13, although this is still a target date, not a confirmed launch date. The mission is called Starship Flight 15. SpaceX's launch tower has already caught a booster once, back in October 2024. But the upper part of Starship, known as the Ship, has never been caught before. So the big question is: how can something this massive be caught in midair?

Paragraph 2

First, let's look at how the launch will happen. Flight 15 will lift off from Pad 2 at Starbase in Texas, using Booster 22 and Ship 42. Both belong to Starship's new V3, or Block 3, generation. Around 6 minutes and 35 seconds after launch, the booster will return to Starbase, where the tower's mechanical arms will attempt to catch it. Meanwhile, the Ship will continue into space and reach an altitude of approximately 275 kilometers. There, it will deploy 26 new Starlink V3 satellites. This will be Starship's second orbital mission, following Flight 14, which took place on September 28.

Paragraph 3

Now comes the real challenge. The Ship will spend approximately 21 hours orbiting Earth, allowing its flight path to bring it back in front of Starbase. During this time, it will complete around 14 orbits, making the entire mission more than 22 hours long. Throughout this period, flight controllers will monitor its engines, power systems, computers, and fuel tanks. The return will only happen if the spacecraft meets all the conditions approved by SpaceX and the FAA. If anything doesn't look right, the Ship will splash down near Hawaii or in the ocean off Chile, bringing the mission to an end. Another major obstacle is bringing the Ship back toward land, which requires it to pass over populated areas. For that, approval of changes to its FAA license is still pending. Until that approval comes through, the plan to catch both stages cannot fly exactly as intended. In October, SpaceX's president said she expected the license to allow catches of both stages.

Paragraph 4

So why is all of this such a big deal? If successful, this will be the first time both parts of Starship are caught during a single flight. It is part of SpaceX's larger plan to increase launch frequency and make its rockets reusable. However, nothing is guaranteed yet, because the date, license, and mission plan could all change.
